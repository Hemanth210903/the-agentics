Retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) improves the reliability of AI text generators by letting them look up information before producing output [source needed]. The system consists of two components: a retriever that scans a document collection and returns a few relevant passages, and a generative language model that conditions its response on those passages. Grounding the answer in retrieved text reduces hallucinations and allows the knowledge base to be updated simply by adding new documents, without retraining the model [source needed].

Empirical results show that this hybrid architecture outperforms standalone generators on knowledge‑intensive tasks. For instance, on the Natural Questions benchmark a RAG model achieved a 12 % higher exact‑match score than the same model without retrieval [source needed]. Fusion‑in‑decoder (FiD) and similar techniques feed each retrieved passage separately into the encoder and merge their representations in the decoder, enabling effective combination of multiple sources [source needed].

Major AI platforms now offer RAG as a built‑in service—Microsoft Azure OpenAI, Anthropic Claude with a search tool, and Cohere’s RAG‑API—allowing developers to build chatbots that answer queries using proprietary knowledge bases [source needed]. Current research focuses on detecting erroneous retrievals and scaling document stores to billions of passages while maintaining low latency [source needed].
